# Machine-Law Primacy Contract

## Document positioning

This file is a root-domain contract law file inside `identity/protocol/`.

It freezes the law that keeps identity protocol machine-law-first, prohibits
active compatibility sheltering, and preserves fail-close exposure over silent
truth laundering.

It is not:

1. a migration cookbook;
2. a backward-compatibility promise;
3. a workspace-local residue waiver;
4. a substitute for current-turn machine adjudication.

## Root-law scope and non-goals

1. This file freezes root-domain machine-law primacy for the machine world.
2. It is not a charter for permanent bridges, legacy overlays, or convenience fallbacks.
3. It does not let compatibility shelters, local vividness, historical residue, or operator comfort outrun law.
4. It must not be treated as a runtime receipt, migration proof, or substitute for machine-consumed enforcement surfaces.

## Purpose

Define why active protocol surfaces remain law-first, what kinds of
compatibility or migration behavior may be demoted away from the success path,
and why missing or ambiguous truth must fail-close rather than be silently
patched.

This file remains the authoritative root-domain contract for machine-law primacy.

Current-turn machine-law primacy legality must still resolve from machine-consumed enforcement surfaces such as:

1. governed governance/review documentation and admitted protocol deltas;

So this file freezes machine-law primacy, while runtime adjudication determines whether that law has actually been satisfied in the present turn.

## Machine-law primacy law

The identity protocol must remain machine law first rather than a compatibility shelter or historical backstop.

Active success paths may preserve governed migration, diagnostics, import, or fixture lanes only as demoted support surfaces, never as silent substitutes for current law.

## Four primacy commitments

### 1. Law before compatibility shelter

The active protocol is an upgrade target and law-bearing system, not a shelter for lagging residue, workspace drift, or historical overlays.

Commitment role: `machine_law_first`.

### 2. Governed success path before convenience permissiveness

Only admitted surfaces may enter the current-turn success path; bridges, fallbacks, fixtures, imports, and diagnostics may assist convergence without silently becoming active defaults.

Commitment role: `success_path_admissibility_first`.

### 3. Fail-close exposure before silent swallowing

When current-turn truth is missing, ambiguous, or unbound, the protocol must fail-close or redirect to governed recovery rather than reconstruct legality through compatibility projection, alias bridges, or local convenience.

Commitment role: `fail_close_before_shelter`.

### 4. Governed convergence before downgrade laundering

Instances and workspaces must converge upward to current law; the protocol must not downgrade, weaken, or reopen active success paths merely to keep residue alive.

Commitment role: `governed_convergence_over_downgrade`.

## Required primacy anchors

The protocol must keep machine-law primacy tied to explicit anchors rather than to convenience memory or historical familiarity.

At minimum, machine-law primacy must remain anchored to:

1. machine law first rather than a compatibility shelter or historical backstop;
2. governed current-turn success path rather than convenience-path permissiveness;
3. fail-close exposure of missing or ambiguous truth rather than silent swallowing through compatibility projection;
4. governed convergence and refreezing rather than residue laundering or protocol downgrade.

When a machine-law primacy claim relies on governed proof, the proof stratum
behind that claim must match the primacy commitment being asserted.

## Machine-law primacy proof discipline

Machine-law primacy claims may be supported only by proof whose stratum matches
the primacy claim being asserted.

### 1. Frozen-law primacy proof

Supports claims that active protocol behavior remained grounded in frozen law
rather than in compatibility residue, migration habit, or local vividness.

Proof role: `frozen_law_primacy_proof`.

### 2. Success-path demotion-boundary proof

Supports claims that migration, import, fixture, fallback, helper, and
diagnostic surfaces remained demoted outside active success-path legality.

Proof role: `success_path_demotion_boundary_primacy_proof`.

### 3. Fail-close exposure proof

Supports claims that missing or ambiguous truth was exposed through lawful
failure or governed recovery rather than reconstructed through compatibility
projection.

Proof role: `fail_close_exposure_primacy_proof`.

### 4. Governed-convergence proof

Supports claims that workspaces and instances were required to converge upward
to law rather than forcing protocol downgrade for residue survival.

Proof role: `governed_convergence_primacy_proof`.

### 5. Runtime-adjudication non-bypass proof

Supports claims that machine-law primacy preserved current-turn machine
adjudication and did not become a freeform waiver or narrative override.

Proof role: `runtime_adjudication_non_bypass_primacy_proof`.

## Machine-law primacy proof limits

The protocol must preserve these machine-law primacy proof limits:

1. frozen-law primacy proof is not proof of success-path demotion boundary;
2. success-path demotion-boundary proof is not proof of fail-close exposure;
3. fail-close exposure proof is not proof of governed convergence;
4. governed-convergence proof is not proof of runtime-adjudication non-bypass;
5. runtime-adjudication non-bypass proof is not proof that compatibility or recovery surfaces may re-enter active success-path legality.

## Non-compliant compatibility collapses

The following are non-compliant:

1. `compatibility_shelter_substitution`: the active protocol is treated as a shelter for lagging residue instead of as the upgrade target.
2. `silent_truth_reconstruction`: missing or ambiguous current-turn truth is rebuilt through compatibility projection, alias bridges, or local familiarity.
3. `demoted_surface_reentry`: migration, import, fixture, diagnostic, or fallback surfaces re-enter active defaults as if demotion did not exist.
4. `protocol_downgrade_for_residue`: shared law is weakened or reopened merely to keep local residue alive.
5. `convenience_claims_legality`: convenience, vividness, or historical familiarity is treated as if it proved active-path legality.
